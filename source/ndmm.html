It is not a clinical practice guideline, medical advice or a substitute for clinical judgment. Patient care should follow the primary literature, current guidelines (such as NCCN, ESMO, IMWG and ASCO), approved prescribing information and the individual patient's circumstances.

Key points

  • Induction has moved from doublets to triplets to anti-CD38 quadruplets, now standard for transplant-eligible patients (PERSEUS) and for fit transplant-ineligible patients (IMROZ, CEPHEUS).
  • Autologous transplant still improves progression-free survival after modern induction, but not overall survival (IFM 2009, DETERMINATION); deferral is reasonable for selected patients.
  • For older or frail patients, daratumumab-lenalidomide-dexamethasone (MAIA) improved overall survival over lenalidomide-dexamethasone and remains a standard regimen.
  • Lenalidomide maintenance is standard after transplant; how long to give it, and when to add a second agent, is still being defined.
  • MRD negativity is now an accepted endpoint for accelerated approval, and a 2025 consensus gives a common genomic definition of high-risk disease.

Two decisions that frame frontline care

For two decades, frontline myeloma therapy has turned on two decisions: whether the patient is a candidate for autologous transplant, and how intensive and how long the treatment around it should be. The answers have shifted as each new drug class moved from relapse into first line.

Transplant-eligible patients

High-dose melphalan with autologous transplant became standard when MRC Myeloma VII improved complete responses (44% vs 8%) and median survival (54.1 vs 42.3 months) over conventional chemotherapy1; a second (tandem) transplant helped mainly patients without a very good partial response after the first2. Induction then improved: bortezomib-dexamethasone replaced VAD3 and the VTD triplet nearly tripled complete or near-complete responses compared with thalidomide-dexamethasone4.

With better induction, the value of transplant was retested. In IFM 2009, transplant after VRd extended median PFS from 36 to 50 months without changing 4-year survival5; EMN02 found a similar PFS advantage over bortezomib-based intensification6; and the US DETERMINATION trial, with lenalidomide until progression, showed median PFS of 67.5 vs 46.2 months and nearly identical 5-year survival (80.7% vs 79.2%)7. Transplant therefore remains standard, while deferral is a reasonable choice for some patients if stem cells are collected.

The anti-CD38 quadruplet era began with CASSIOPEIA, where daratumumab added to VTd raised MRD negativity from 44% to 64%8. GRIFFIN brought daratumumab-VRd into US practice9, and PERSEUS confirmed it: 4-year PFS 84.3% vs 67.7% (HR 0.42)10. Isatuximab-VRd raised MRD negativity after induction in GMMG-HD711. Carfilzomib-based regimens deepened responses further: KRd induction and carfilzomib-lenalidomide maintenance prolonged PFS12, isatuximab-KRd increased MRD negativity at 10⁻⁶ (68% vs 48%)13, and MASTER tested MRD-guided stopping, which worked in standard-risk but not ultra-high-risk disease14.

Transplant-ineligible patients

For older patients, adding thalidomide to melphalan-prednisone extended median survival from 33.2 to 51.6 months15, and bortezomib-melphalan-prednisone improved time to progression16. Continuous lenalidomide-dexamethasone then provided an alkylator-free backbone17, and adding bortezomib (VRd) improved both PFS and overall survival (median 75 vs 64 months)18. Substituting carfilzomib for bortezomib did not help in standard-risk patients19.

Anti-CD38 antibodies changed outcomes again. Daratumumab improved PFS with VMP20 and, with lenalidomide-dexamethasone in MAIA, improved overall survival (HR 0.68), a regimen that remains a standard for older or frail patients21. For fit patients not proceeding to transplant, quadruplets followed: isatuximab-VRd in patients up to 80 years achieved 5-year PFS of 63.2% vs 45.2%22, and daratumumab-VRd raised MRD negativity from 39.4% to 60.9%23.

Maintenance

Lenalidomide maintenance after transplant nearly doubled time to progression in CALGB 10010424 and PFS in IFM 2005-02, at the cost of more second primary cancers25; Myeloma XI showed benefit with and without transplant26. A 2026 trial in standard-risk patients without upfront transplant found no survival difference between indefinite and 2-year lenalidomide (7-year overall survival 68.6% vs 69.0%)27.

Risk and response

Staging moved from the International Staging System28 to the Revised ISS, which added cytogenetics and LDH29, and then to R2-ISS30. In 2025, an IMS/IMWG consensus proposed a shared genomic definition of high risk31. Response assessment now centers on MRD: in a meta-analysis, MRD negativity was associated with better PFS (HR 0.41) and survival (HR 0.57), and in April 2024 the FDA's advisory committee voted unanimously to support MRD as an accelerated-approval endpoint32.

Where we are now

NCCN (version 5.2026) and the 2026 ASCO-Ontario living guideline, summarized here at a high level (regimen-specific NCCN placement not checked), recommend:

  • Transplant-eligible patients: anti-CD38 quadruplet induction (daratumumab-VRd, or isatuximab-VRd where approved), autologous transplant, and lenalidomide maintenance, with daratumumab continued as in PERSEUS; carfilzomib-containing maintenance may be considered in selected high-risk patients.
  • Fit transplant-ineligible or deferred patients: anti-CD38 quadruplets (isatuximab-VRd or daratumumab-VRd).
  • Older or frail patients: daratumumab-lenalidomide-dexamethasone.
  • Everyone: staging with R-ISS or R2-ISS, high-risk assessment by genomics, and MRD monitoring at defined time points.

Open questions

  • Transplant in deep responders: can patients who are MRD-negative after a quadruplet safely skip or defer transplant?
  • Duration: can MRD guide when to stop maintenance?
  • High-risk disease: outcomes remain inferior despite quadruplets and intensified maintenance.
  • Choice of antibody and backbone: daratumumab versus isatuximab, and bortezomib versus carfilzomib.
  • Immunotherapy in first line: whether CAR-T or bispecific antibodies should replace transplant or maintenance.

What is coming

Ongoing phase 3 trials (listed under Trials to watch at the end of this page) test CAR-T in first line (CARTITUDE-5 and CARTITUDE-6), bispecific antibodies in induction and maintenance (MajesTEC-7, MagnetisMM-6, MajesTEC-4, MagnetisMM-7), belantamab with lenalidomide (DREAMM-10), iberdomide maintenance, fixed-duration versus continuous anti-CD38 therapy, and response-adapted quadruplets.

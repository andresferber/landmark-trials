Landmark trials in untreated chronic lymphocytic leukemia

From chemoimmunotherapy to continuous BTK inhibitors and fixed-duration venetoclax combinations, with the risk models and criteria that decide when to treat: design, verified results, context, open questions and reception.

Key points

  • Asymptomatic early-stage CLL is still observed: ibrutinib delayed progression but did not improve survival (CLL12).
  • Chemoimmunotherapy peaked with FCR, which improved survival in fit patients and gives very long remissions in IGHV-mutated disease.
  • Continuous BTK inhibitors replaced chemoimmunotherapy at every age; ibrutinib-rituximab improved survival over FCR in E1912.
  • One year of venetoclax-obinutuzumab gave a fixed-duration alternative, first in patients with comorbidities (CLL14) and then in fit patients (CLL13).
  • All-oral fixed-duration combinations of a BTK inhibitor with venetoclax followed; acalabrutinib-venetoclax is the one approved in the US.
  • In the first direct comparison (CLL17), fixed-duration regimens were non-inferior to continuous ibrutinib at 3 years, so the choice rests on TP53 and IGHV status, comorbidities and preference.

A disease treated only when it causes problems

Most patients with CLL are diagnosed without symptoms, and many never need treatment. That principle has survived every new drug. Genetic testing by FISH separated patients with a 17p deletion, whose median survival was 32 months, from those with a 13q deletion alone, at 133 months1. The CLL-IPI added TP53 and IGHV status to stage, age and β2-microglobulin to predict survival2, and the IPS-E predicts how soon an early-stage patient will need treatment3. The iwCLL criteria define active disease, the indication to start4. When early treatment was tested with a modern drug, ibrutinib delayed progression in higher-risk Binet A disease but survival was the same, 93.3% vs 93.6% at 5 years5.

The chemoimmunotherapy era

Fludarabine gave more and longer responses than chlorambucil without improving survival6, and adding cyclophosphamide raised 5-year PFS from 10% to 36%, again without a survival gain7. Rituximab changed that. FCR produced complete responses in 70% of patients in its phase 2 trial8, and in CLL8 it improved both PFS and overall survival over FC9. With long follow-up, about half of patients with mutated IGHV remained progression-free beyond 12 years, with a plateau that still argues for FCR in a few selected patients8.

FCR was too toxic for most patients, who are older or have comorbidities. Bendamustine was better than chlorambucil10, and with rituximab it was less toxic than FCR but gave shorter PFS in fit patients, 41.7 vs 55.2 months11. For patients with comorbidities, obinutuzumab-chlorambucil gave a median PFS of 26.7 months and became the standard, and the comparator for the trials that followed1213.

Continuous BTK inhibitors

Ibrutinib moved to first-line treatment with RESONATE-2, where it improved PFS and survival over chlorambucil; in the final analysis median PFS was 8.9 years14. It then beat each chemoimmunotherapy standard: bendamustine-rituximab in older patients, where rituximab added nothing to ibrutinib15; chlorambucil-obinutuzumab16; and FCR in younger patients, with better overall survival in E191217 but not in FLAIR, which also reported more sudden cardiac deaths with ibrutinib18.

The more selective inhibitors followed the same path. Acalabrutinib, with or without obinutuzumab, was better than chlorambucil-obinutuzumab, and after 6 years the combination had longer PFS than acalabrutinib alone and longer survival than chemoimmunotherapy19. Zanubrutinib was better than bendamustine-rituximab, with a 5-year hazard ratio for PFS of 0.2920. Pirtobrutinib, a noncovalent inhibitor, has now done the same21. The trials that compared BTK inhibitors with each other (ELEVATE-RR, ALPINE and BRUIN CLL-314) were run mainly in relapsed disease and belong to the relapsed CLL topic; they are the reason acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib are generally preferred to ibrutinib.

Fixed-duration venetoclax combinations

CLL14 gave 12 cycles of venetoclax-obinutuzumab to patients with comorbidities. After 6 years, median PFS was 76.2 months against 36.4 with chlorambucil-obinutuzumab, most of it off treatment22. CLL13 extended the regimen to fit patients: venetoclax-obinutuzumab, with or without ibrutinib, was better than FCR or bendamustine-rituximab, while venetoclax-rituximab was not23, and CRISTALLO confirmed the deeper responses24.

Combining a BTK inhibitor with venetoclax gave an all-oral regimen. CAPTIVATE defined it25; GLOW showed better PFS and, with long follow-up, better survival than chlorambucil-obinutuzumab in older patients26; and FLAIR, which adjusted duration to the MRD response, reported a 5-year PFS of 93.9% against 79.0% with ibrutinib alone and 58.1% with FCR27. Ibrutinib-venetoclax was not approved in the United States. AMPLIFY tested acalabrutinib-venetoclax for 14 cycles, with a 36-month PFS of 76.5% against 66.5% with chemoimmunotherapy, and led to the US approval of that combination in 202628.

Fixed duration or continuous?

CLL17 is the first trial to compare the two strategies directly. At 3 years, PFS was 81.1% with venetoclax-obinutuzumab, 79.4% with venetoclax-ibrutinib and 81.0% with continuous ibrutinib, and both fixed-duration regimens met non-inferiority29. The follow-up is short, and the answer may differ by subgroup: after venetoclax-obinutuzumab, remissions are shorter with del(17p) and unmutated IGHV22, while with continuous ibrutinib PFS was not inferior in patients with TP53 abnormalities15.

Where we are now

This is a personal summary, not a guideline; NCCN placements were not checked against the current document.

  • Asymptomatic early stage: observation, whatever the risk factors5.
  • Before treatment: FISH for del(17p), TP53 sequencing and IGHV mutation status4.
  • Needs treatment, no TP53 aberration: a choice between a continuous BTK inhibitor (acalabrutinib, zanubrutinib or pirtobrutinib) and a fixed-duration regimen (venetoclax-obinutuzumab or acalabrutinib-venetoclax), by comorbidities, IGHV status, logistics and preference.
  • del(17p) or TP53 mutation: a continuous BTK inhibitor is the usual choice; most fixed-duration trials excluded these patients.
  • Chemoimmunotherapy: FCR only for selected young, fit patients with IGHV-mutated disease and no TP53 aberration who want a single short treatment.
  • Maintenance and transplant: not part of first-line treatment today. Allogeneic transplant is reserved for relapsed high-risk disease and Richter transformation.

Open questions

  • Fixed duration or continuous therapy: longer follow-up of CLL17, by IGHV and TP53 status, and how well each strategy can be repeated at relapse.
  • Which fixed-duration regimen: venetoclax with obinutuzumab or with a BTK inhibitor, and whether triplets are worth their toxicity2328.
  • MRD-guided duration: effective in FLAIR, but not yet tested with the newer BTK inhibitors.
  • TP53-aberrant disease: still the group with the shortest remissions and the least randomized evidence. A nonrandomized cohort of zanubrutinib-venetoclax enrolled 66 patients with TP53 aberration among 114, with an estimated 24-month PFS of 92%30; it has no randomized comparator.
  • Early treatment: whether a fixed-duration regimen in the highest-risk asymptomatic patients improves survival.
  • Richter transformation: not covered here; it will be a separate section built later.

What is coming

Ongoing phase 3 trials (listed under Trials to watch) compare acalabrutinib-venetoclax with venetoclax-obinutuzumab (MAJIC), test sonrotoclax with zanubrutinib against venetoclax-obinutuzumab, against venetoclax-acalabrutinib and against continuous zanubrutinib, and ask whether venetoclax added to ibrutinib-obinutuzumab helps (A041702, EA9161). CLL16 tests a triplet in high-risk disease, EVOLVE CLL/SLL tests early venetoclax-obinutuzumab in high-risk asymptomatic patients, BELLWAVE-011 compares nemtabrutinib with covalent BTK inhibitors, and SOUNDTRACK-C1 tests consolidation with a bispecific antibody. BTK degraders are in phase 3 in relapsed disease (see New drugs to watch).

Key points

  • Adding rituximab to CHOP improved survival at every age and risk level, and R-CHOP every 21 days for 6 cycles became the reference regimen.
  • For 15 years nothing improved on it: dose density, a different anti-CD20 antibody, infusional chemotherapy, upfront transplant and maintenance all failed to improve survival.
  • Less treatment is enough for favourable limited-stage disease: 4 cycles of R-CHOP, guided by risk factors or by interim PET.
  • Adding a targeted drug chosen by cell of origin (bortezomib, ibrutinib, lenalidomide) failed in phase 3 trials.
  • Polatuzumab vedotin in place of vincristine (POLARIX) and tafasitamab-lenalidomide added to R-CHOP (frontMIND) each improved PFS, so far without an overall survival benefit.

A curable disease with one regimen for twenty years

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma is curable with first-line treatment in most patients, and those who are not cured do poorly. The history of first-line therapy since 2000 has three parts: the arrival of rituximab, a long series of failed attempts to improve on R-CHOP, and, since 2022, the first positive add-on trials.

Rituximab changes the standard

In patients aged 60 to 80, adding rituximab to 8 cycles of CHOP raised the complete response rate from 63% to 76% and improved survival, and at 10 years overall survival was 43.5% vs 27.6%1. The US intergroup trial confirmed the benefit in older patients and showed that rituximab maintenance adds nothing after R-CHOP2. MInT extended the result to young patients with good-prognosis disease, with a 3-year event-free survival of 79% vs 59%3, and RICOVER-60 showed that six cycles were as good as eight4.

More intensity did not help

Giving R-CHOP every 14 days was no better than every 21 days, in adults of all ages5 and in older patients6. Extra rituximab early in treatment did not improve outcome7, and the glycoengineered antibody obinutuzumab was not better than rituximab8. Infusional dose-adjusted EPOCH-R was more toxic than R-CHOP and no more effective9. High-dose therapy with autologous transplant in first remission was not superior to conventional therapy in young high-risk patients10, and in SWOG S9704 it improved 2-year PFS (69% vs 55%) but not overall survival11. The one exception was R-ACVBP, which improved 3-year overall survival (92% vs 84%) in patients under 60 with an age-adjusted IPI of 1, at the cost of more toxicity12.

Less treatment for favourable disease, and adapted treatment for the very old

In non-bulky limited-stage disease, radiotherapy after R-CHOP did not improve event-free survival13. In young patients with no risk factors, 4 cycles of R-CHOP were non-inferior to 6, with a 3-year PFS of 96%14. PET-directed therapy gave similar results without an age limit in a US trial15, and a randomized trial in low-risk patients up to age 80 showed that stopping at 4 cycles after a negative PET was non-inferior to 6 cycles16. Interim PET predicts outcome, but intensifying chemotherapy for PET-positive patients did not help17.

For patients over 80, reduced-dose R-miniCHOP gave a 2-year overall survival of 59% and became the standard18; adding lenalidomide to it added toxicity without benefit19. Lenalidomide maintenance after R-CHOP prolonged PFS in patients aged 60 to 80 but not survival, and was not adopted20.

Biology: from cell of origin to genetic subtypes

Gene-expression profiling divided DLBCL into germinal centre and activated B-cell-like disease, with different survival21, and an immunohistochemical algorithm brought the distinction into routine pathology22. Clinical risk is still estimated with the IPI and its revisions2324, and CNS risk with the CNS-IPI25. MYC rearrangement carries a poor prognosis mainly when combined with BCL2 or BCL6 rearrangement and an immunoglobulin partner26. Genomic studies have since defined genetic subtypes that cut across cell of origin27.

Acting on cell of origin was disappointing. Bortezomib added to R-CHOP did not improve PFS, although a later analysis suggested a benefit in activated B-cell and molecular high-grade disease28. Ibrutinib did not improve event-free survival in non-GCB disease, helping patients under 60 and harming older ones29, and lenalidomide did not improve PFS in activated B-cell disease30.

The first positive add-on trials

POLARIX replaced vincristine with polatuzumab vedotin in patients with an IPI of 2 to 5: 2-year PFS was 76.7% vs 70.2% (HR 0.73), and at 5 years 64.9% vs 59.1%, without a significant difference in overall survival31. frontMIND added tafasitamab and lenalidomide to R-CHOP in high-intermediate and high-risk disease: 2-year PFS was 71.1% vs 62.9% (HR 0.75), with more grade 3 or higher adverse events (87% vs 76%) and immature survival data32. In double-expressor lymphoma, a trial in China reported better event-free survival with the histone deacetylase inhibitor tucidinostat added to R-CHOP (HR 0.72)33. First-line CAR-T has been tested only in a single-arm trial in high-risk disease, with complete responses in 78%34.

Where we are now

Summarized at a high level; NCCN placement was not checked against the current document.

  • Favourable limited stage: 4 cycles of R-CHOP, by risk factors14 or interim PET1516, usually without radiotherapy.
  • Advanced stage or IPI 2 or higher: 6 cycles of R-CHOP or pola-R-CHP31.
  • Over 80 or frail: R-miniCHOP18.
  • At diagnosis: IPI, cell of origin, FISH for MYC rearrangement, and an estimate of CNS risk25.

Open questions

  • Who benefits from pola-R-CHP: no overall survival benefit so far, and exploratory analyses point to activated B-cell disease and higher IPI.
  • Which add-on: polatuzumab and tafasitamab-lenalidomide have not been compared, and combinations with bispecific antibodies are in phase 3.
  • Selection by biology: whether genetic subtypes will succeed where cell of origin failed.
  • CNS prophylaxis: the CNS-IPI identifies risk, but no intervention is proven to lower it.
  • High-grade and double-hit lymphoma: treated with intensive regimens on non-randomized data.

What is coming

Ongoing phase 3 trials (listed under Trials to watch) add CD20×CD3 bispecific antibodies to first-line chemoimmunotherapy: glofitamab with pola-R-CHP (GO44145), epcoritamab with R-CHOP (EPCORE DLBCL-2) and odronextamab with CHOP (OLYMPIA-3). Others test golcadomide (GOLSEEK-1), acalabrutinib in non-GCB disease (ESCALADE) and zilovertamab vedotin (waveLINE-010) with R-CHOP or R-CHP, first-line axicabtagene ciloleucel in high-risk disease (ZUMA-23), and polatuzumab or acalabrutinib with reduced-dose chemotherapy in older patients (POLAR BEAR, ARCHED).

Relapsed/refractory chronic lymphocytic leukemia · 2002–2026

Landmark trials in relapsed and refractory chronic lymphocytic leukemia

From chemoimmunotherapy and antibodies to BTK inhibitors and their head-to-head trials, venetoclax-based fixed-duration therapy, the rise and fall of PI3K inhibitors, and treatment after both a BTK and a BCL2 inhibitor: design, verified results, context, open questions and reception.

Key points

  • Before targeted therapy, relapsed CLL was treated with chemoimmunotherapy or antibodies, with short remissions, especially in fludarabine-refractory and del(17p) disease.
  • Ibrutinib improved survival over ofatumumab (RESONATE). The more selective covalent BTK inhibitors are as effective or more, with fewer cardiac events (ELEVATE-RR, ALPINE).
  • Two years of venetoclax-rituximab established fixed-duration treatment at relapse and improved survival over bendamustine-rituximab (MURANO).
  • PI3K inhibitors prolonged PFS, but infections, immune-mediated toxicity and a possible survival detriment with duvelisib left them a late-line role at most.
  • After a covalent BTK inhibitor, the options are venetoclax-based therapy or pirtobrutinib; after both classes, pirtobrutinib if not yet used, or CAR T cells. Remissions there are still short.
  • Most patients now receive a BTK inhibitor or venetoclax as first treatment, so the choice at relapse depends mainly on what was given before and why it was stopped.

Before targeted therapy

Until 2013, treatment of relapsed CLL repeated what had been used first line. In fit patients, adding rituximab to fludarabine and cyclophosphamide lengthened median PFS from 20.6 to 30.6 months1. Once fludarabine had failed, the options were antibodies. Alemtuzumab gave responses in 33% of patients, with a median time to progression of 4.7 months2, and ofatumumab gave a median PFS under 6 months in disease refractory to fludarabine3. These numbers are the background against which the targeted drugs were judged, and ofatumumab became their comparator.

BTK inhibitors

Ibrutinib changed the outlook within two years. In its phase 1b/2 trial, 71% of heavily pretreated patients responded, with or without del(17p), and 75% were progression-free at 26 months4. RESONATE then showed better PFS and overall survival than ofatumumab; in the final analysis median PFS was 44.1 months against 8.15. Adding ibrutinib to bendamustine-rituximab also improved PFS and survival, but HELIOS had no ibrutinib-alone arm, and the combination did not replace ibrutinib alone6.

Continuous ibrutinib had two limits. One was toxicity: in the final RESONATE analysis, 21% of patients had hypertension and 12% atrial fibrillation5. The other was resistance. Sequencing at relapse found a mutation of BTK at the cysteine where the drug binds (C481S) in 5 of 6 patients, and mutations of PLCG2, just downstream, in 27. That finding is the rationale for the noncovalent inhibitors and the BTK degraders that came later.

Which BTK inhibitor?

Acalabrutinib was designed to be more selective. After a phase 1/2 trial with a response rate of 95%8, ASCEND showed longer PFS than idelalisib-rituximab or bendamustine-rituximab, with a 42-month PFS of 62% against 19%9. Three trials then compared BTK inhibitors directly. In ELEVATE-RR, in patients with del(17p) or del(11q), acalabrutinib was not inferior to ibrutinib, with a median PFS of 38.4 months in both arms and less atrial fibrillation, 9.4% against 16.0%10. In ALPINE, zanubrutinib gave longer PFS than ibrutinib, with a hazard ratio of 0.68 at 42.5 months, and fewer cardiac events11. In BRUIN CLL-314, which enrolled both relapsed and untreated patients, the response rate with the noncovalent inhibitor pirtobrutinib was not inferior to that with ibrutinib, again with less atrial fibrillation and hypertension; its PFS data are not mature12.

These trials are the reason acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib are generally preferred to ibrutinib, in relapsed and in untreated disease. Acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib have not been compared with each other.

Venetoclax and fixed-duration treatment

Venetoclax kills CLL cells by inhibiting BCL2, without needing p53. In its phase 1 trial 79% of patients responded and 20% reached complete remission; tumor lysis syndrome in the first cohorts led to the weekly dose ramp-up still in use13. Given continuously to patients with del(17p), it produced responses in 79.4%, and with six years of follow-up median PFS was 28.2 months14. MURANO turned it into a fixed-duration treatment: two years of venetoclax with rituximab for the first six months gave a 2-year PFS of 84.9% against 36.3% with bendamustine-rituximab. In the final analysis, median PFS was 54.7 months against 17.0, and 7-year survival 69.6% against 51.0%15. Patients with undetectable MRD at the end of treatment had the longest remissions, and retreatment with the same regimen produced responses in 72% of 25 patients15.

MURANO enrolled almost no patients previously treated with a BTK inhibitor. The evidence that venetoclax works after ibrutinib came from a phase 2 trial, with a response rate of 65%16, and from VENICE-1, where complete remission was reached by 35% of patients not previously treated with a B-cell receptor pathway inhibitor and 27% of those who were17. A phase 1b trial then combined pirtobrutinib with venetoclax for a fixed 25 cycles, with or without rituximab, with responses in 93.3% and 100% of 25 patients18. BRUIN CLL-322 took the triplet to a randomized trial against venetoclax-rituximab, in which 80% of patients had received a covalent BTK inhibitor: adding pirtobrutinib raised 24-month PFS from 72% to 87%19. Whether the doublet without rituximab does as well is being studied.

PI3K inhibitors: a cautionary story

Idelalisib with rituximab was highly effective in frail patients with relapsed CLL, with a hazard ratio for progression of 0.15 and better survival at 12 months than rituximab alone20. But combining it with bendamustine-rituximab or with ofatumumab brought more serious infections and more deaths from adverse events2122, and in ASCEND two thirds of patients stopped idelalisib-rituximab because of adverse events9. Duvelisib improved PFS over ofatumumab, 13.3 against 9.9 months23; in 2022 the FDA warned that the final survival analysis pointed the other way, and its advisory committee judged the balance of benefit and risk unfavorable. A third drug of the class, umbralisib, was tested with ublituximab in UNITY-CLL, which has not been published in full; umbralisib was withdrawn in 2022. The lesson went beyond CLL: PFS can improve while survival does not, and drugs for a disease with long survival need randomized data on overall survival.

After a BTK inhibitor, and after both classes

Pirtobrutinib binds BTK reversibly, at a site not affected by the usual resistance mutation. In patients already treated with a BTK inhibitor, 73.3% responded and median PFS was 19.6 months24. The randomized trial that followed, in patients all previously treated with a covalent BTK inhibitor, showed a median PFS of 14 months against 8.7 with idelalisib-rituximab or bendamustine-rituximab, with no difference in survival25. The second number matters as much as the first: older regimens do little after a BTK inhibitor.

For patients whose disease has progressed on a BTK inhibitor and after venetoclax, lisocabtagene maraleucel produced complete remissions in 18%, and in a later cohort given with ibrutinib in 45%26. Allogeneic transplant remains an option for selected fit patients at this stage and for Richter transformation, which is not covered here and will be a separate section. Maintenance after chemoimmunotherapy, with lenalidomide or an anti-CD20 antibody, prolonged PFS in randomized trials but lost its place when continuous and fixed-duration targeted therapy arrived.

Where we are now

This is a personal summary, not a guideline; NCCN placements were not checked against the current document.

  • Before treatment: repeat FISH for del(17p) and TP53 sequencing, since both can be acquired; relapse alone is not an indication to treat.
  • After chemoimmunotherapy only: a continuous BTK inhibitor (acalabrutinib or zanubrutinib) or fixed-duration venetoclax-rituximab, chosen as in first-line treatment.
  • After a fixed-duration venetoclax regimen with a long remission: a BTK inhibitor, or venetoclax-based retreatment15.
  • After a covalent BTK inhibitor stopped for intolerance: another BTK inhibitor or venetoclax-based therapy.
  • After progression on a covalent BTK inhibitor: venetoclax-based therapy or pirtobrutinib1625.
  • After both a covalent BTK inhibitor and venetoclax: pirtobrutinib if not yet used, lisocabtagene maraleucel, a clinical trial, and transplant evaluation in fit patients2426.
  • PI3K inhibitors and chemoimmunotherapy: late-line options at most.

Open questions

  • Sequence: venetoclax-based therapy or pirtobrutinib first after a covalent BTK inhibitor? No trial has compared them.
  • Pirtobrutinib earlier: if a noncovalent inhibitor is used first, or in a fixed-duration triplet, what works at the next relapse1219?
  • Retreatment: how often, and after how long a remission, can a fixed-duration regimen be repeated?
  • Disease refractory to both classes: remissions are short with every current option; this is where BTK degraders and other new agents are being tested.
  • Survival: most recent trials show PFS gains without survival differences, with crossover; the PI3K story is the reason to keep asking for survival data.
  • Richter transformation: not covered here; it will be a separate section built later.

What is coming

Ongoing phase 3 trials (listed under Trials to watch) test BTK degraders against pirtobrutinib (BGB-16673-304, NX-5948-306) and against older regimens after both drug classes (CaDAnCe-302), the BCL2 inhibitor sonrotoclax with an anti-CD20 antibody against venetoclax-rituximab (BGB-11417-303), and the noncovalent BTK inhibitor nemtabrutinib with venetoclax against venetoclax-rituximab (BELLWAVE-010). Early data on these agents are under New drugs to watch.

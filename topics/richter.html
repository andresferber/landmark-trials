Richter transformation of CLL · first-line and relapsed · 2003–2026

Landmark studies in Richter transformation

Initial treatment and relapse in one topic, because no treatment is established at either point: risk, biology and diagnosis, chemoimmunotherapy and transplantation, BTK, BCL2 and checkpoint inhibitors, bispecific antibodies and CAR T cells, and the Hodgkin variant. There are no randomized trials yet; most entries are phase 2 trials and retrospective series, each with its design, verified results, context, open questions and reception.

"Key toxicity" summarizes the main adverse events reported in the abstract; it is not a complete safety profile. "Drug mechanism" links one recent review per drug or drug class on how it works and how resistance develops, chosen from peer-reviewed journals as the most recent, comprehensive and high-quality review available (no promotional material), with free full text preferred when quality was comparable. "★ Core" marks studies selected as essential for teaching. Studies included: Richter transformation is rare and has no randomized trials, so the usual rule for this site (phase 3 trials and selected phase 2 trials) would leave almost nothing. This topic therefore lists the prospective trials published in Richter transformation, whatever the journal, the larger retrospective, registry and genomic studies that shape prognosis and practice, and the consensus and guideline documents. First-line and relapsed disease are covered together. Small case series, single-agent reports with fewer than 9 patients and reports published only as meeting abstracts are not listed. Each result is tagged "✓ abstract" or "✓ abstract + full paper" by what it was checked against. "Free full text" links open the article in PubMed Central; "Free at publisher" links open a publisher page that is free to read; "Publisher" links open the publisher's page, where a subscription or institutional access may be needed; "Free copy (repository)" links open a legal free copy in a university repository (sometimes the authors' manuscript). Free copies were found with Unpaywall (November 12, 2026). Registry links (NCT numbers) open the trial's registry record and were taken from each paper's PubMed record, abstract or full text. Source links go to the PubMed record (PMIDs checked against journal, volume, pages and first author). "Builds on", "Changed" and "Open questions" are interpretive summaries, not data from the papers. Commentaries are those PubMed links to each paper ("Comment in"); letters and author replies are not listed. No drug has an approval specific to Richter transformation; regulatory notes say so for each agent, and one item marked "verify" will be checked at the next update. Guideline statements from the EHA 2026 guidelines were checked against their full text; placements marked "NCCN not checked" could not be confirmed, because the NCCN guidelines require a login.

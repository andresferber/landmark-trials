Landmark trials in relapsed and refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

Key points

  • For patients relapsing after R-CHOP, salvage chemotherapy and autologous transplant cured few of those with refractory disease or relapse within 12 months.
  • CD19 CAR-T changed this: first in third line on single-arm trials, then in second line, where axicabtagene ciloleucel (ZUMA-7) improved overall survival over salvage and transplant.
  • One of the three second-line CAR-T trials was negative (BELINDA), a reminder that products and trial designs are not interchangeable.
  • CD20×CD3 bispecific antibodies give complete responses in about 30–40% of patients in third line, including after CAR-T, and their combinations beat R-GemOx in randomized trials.
  • Antibody-drug conjugates and other targeted agents add options, with survival benefits for polatuzumab vedotin and brentuximab vedotin combinations in phase 3 trials.

The problem after R-CHOP

Before 2017, the only curative treatment for relapsed diffuse large B-cell lymphoma was salvage chemotherapy followed by high-dose therapy and autologous transplant, for patients fit enough and whose disease still responded to chemotherapy. Once rituximab was part of first-line therapy, this approach worked much less well.

Salvage chemotherapy and transplant

Adding rituximab to ICE raised the complete response rate before transplant to 53%, against 27% in historical controls1. CORAL then compared R-ICE with R-DHAP and found no difference; what mattered was the patient's history. Three-year event-free survival was 21% in those previously given rituximab against 47% in those who had not, and 20% for relapse within 12 months against 45% for later relapse, and rituximab maintenance after transplant added nothing2. R-GDP was as effective as R-DHAP and less toxic3, and replacing rituximab with ofatumumab did not help4. For patients not eligible for transplant, R-GemOx gave responses in 61% but a 5-year PFS of 12.8%5. The pooled SCHOLAR-1 analysis set the benchmark for refractory disease: a response rate of 26% to the next therapy and a median survival of 6.3 months6.

CAR-T in third line

Against that benchmark, three single-arm trials of CD19 CAR-T changed practice. Axicabtagene ciloleucel gave complete responses in 54% of patients with refractory disease, and at 5 years overall survival was 42.6%7. Tisagenlecleucel gave complete responses in 40%, with a 5-year overall survival of 32%8. Lisocabtagene maraleucel gave complete responses in 53%, with grade 3 or higher cytokine release syndrome in 2% and neurological events in 10%9.

CAR-T in second line

Three randomized trials then compared CAR-T with salvage chemotherapy and transplant in the group identified by CORAL: primary refractory disease or relapse within 12 months. In ZUMA-7, axicabtagene ciloleucel improved event-free survival (HR 0.40) and, with longer follow-up, overall survival: 54.6% vs 46.0% at 4 years (HR 0.73)10. In TRANSFORM, lisocabtagene maraleucel improved event-free survival (HR 0.375 at 3 years); overall survival was not significantly different, with 66% of control patients crossing over11. BELINDA, with tisagenlecleucel, was negative: median event-free survival was 3.0 months in both arms, with a median of 52 days from leukapheresis to infusion12. Two single-arm trials extended second-line CAR-T to patients not eligible for transplant, with an overall response rate of 80% with lisocabtagene maraleucel13 and a complete metabolic response rate of 71.0% with axicabtagene ciloleucel14.

Bispecific antibodies

CD20×CD3 bispecific antibodies redirect the patient's own T cells without cell manufacturing. After two or more lines, fixed-duration glofitamab gave complete responses in 39%, including 35% of patients previously given CAR-T15; subcutaneous epcoritamab gave complete responses in 38.9%, with a median duration of complete response of 36.1 months at 3 years16; and odronextamab gave complete responses in 31.5% and is approved in the EU but not in the US17.

Two randomized trials in transplant-ineligible patients after one or more lines used R-GemOx as the control. Glofitamab with gemcitabine-oxaliplatin improved overall survival (HR 0.59), although the FDA did not approve the combination in second line18. Mosunetuzumab with polatuzumab vedotin, without chemotherapy, improved median PFS from 3.8 to 11.5 months (HR 0.41)19.

Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted agents

Polatuzumab vedotin with bendamustine-rituximab improved complete response, PFS and overall survival in a randomized phase 2 trial of 80 patients20, and a phase 3 trial later confirmed a survival benefit when it was added to R-GemOx (median 19.5 vs 12.5 months), with more fatal adverse events (12% vs 4%)21. Single-arm trials led to accelerated approvals of tafasitamab with lenalidomide (ORR 60%)22, loncastuximab tesirine (ORR 48.3%)23 and selinexor (ORR 28%)24. Brentuximab vedotin added to lenalidomide-rituximab improved overall survival in heavily pretreated patients (13.8 vs 8.5 months)25.

Ibrutinib showed that a targeted drug can be selective for a molecular subtype, with responses in 37% of activated B-cell-like and 5% of germinal centre disease26, and the five-drug ViPOR regimen gave complete responses only in non-GCB and double-hit disease27. Pixantrone, a chemotherapy drug approved conditionally in the EU, is now rarely used28.

Where we are now

Summarized at a high level; NCCN placement was not checked against the current document.

  • Primary refractory disease or relapse within 12 months, fit for CAR-T: axicabtagene ciloleucel or lisocabtagene maraleucel in second line1011.
  • Relapse after 12 months, transplant-eligible: platinum-based salvage and autologous transplant if the disease responds23.
  • Not eligible for transplant: second-line lisocabtagene maraleucel13, or tafasitamab-lenalidomide, polatuzumab-based therapy or a bispecific combination where available.
  • Third line and beyond: CAR-T if not yet given; otherwise glofitamab or epcoritamab, loncastuximab tesirine, or brentuximab vedotin with lenalidomide-rituximab25.

Open questions

  • CAR-T or bispecific first: no randomized comparison, and the best order is unknown.
  • Late relapse: whether CAR-T should replace transplant for relapse after 12 months has not been tested in a randomized trial.
  • After polatuzumab in first line: most relapse trials enrolled patients who had not received it.
  • Sharing targets: the effect of CD19- and CD20-directed therapy on later treatments aimed at the same antigens.
  • Access: CAR-T needs specialized centers and time, and STARGLO showed how results from one region may not be accepted in another.

What is coming

Ongoing phase 3 trials (listed under Trials to watch) compare epcoritamab with chemotherapy (EPCORE DLBCL-1) and epcoritamab-lenalidomide with R-GemOx (EPCORE DLBCL-4), odronextamab with standard care (OLYMPIA-4), loncastuximab tesirine with rituximab against immunochemotherapy (LOTIS-5), selinexor added to R-GDP (XPORT-DLBCL-030) and zilovertamab vedotin with standard care (waveLINE-003). A dual CD19/CD20 CAR-T is being compared directly with CD19 CAR-T (PiNACLE-H2H).
